Beverage Hills: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 7 October 2026

This policy explains what information the mobile game Beverage Hills: Escape from Al-can-traz ("the game", "we") uses, why, and the choices you have. The game is made by Jamie Rose, an independent developer based in the United Kingdom.

In short: we don't ask for your name, email or any account. The game shows adverts through Google AdMob and sells an optional "Remove ads" purchase through Google Play and the Apple App Store. Those services collect some information, described below.

1. Information stored on your device only

The game keeps a few things on your phone or tablet so it works as expected. They are never sent to us:

  • Your high-score table and the name you type in for it
  • Your best score and sound setting
  • Whether you have bought "Remove ads"

Deleting the game removes this information.

2. Advertising (Google AdMob)

Unless you have bought "Remove ads", the game shows full-screen adverts between levels using Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, Google may collect:

  • Your device's advertising ID
  • Your IP address, used to estimate your general location
  • Device and app information, such as the device model, operating system and how ads are interacted with

Your choices: if you are in the UK, the EEA or Switzerland, the game asks for your consent before personalised ads are shown, and you can choose not to consent. If you don't consent, Google may still show non-personalised ads. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your device settings (Android: Settings → Google → Ads; iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking).

Google's use of this information is covered by the Google Privacy Policy. See also how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

3. In-app purchase ("Remove ads")

Payments are handled entirely by Google Play or the Apple App Store. We never see your card or payment details.

To check whether you have bought "Remove ads" (so ads stay switched off, including after you reinstall), the game uses RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives a random anonymous ID created by the game, your purchase records from the store, and basic device information such as the device type, operating system and country. It does not receive your name or email address.

5. Legal basis (UK GDPR / EU GDPR)

  • Personalised advertising: your consent, which you can refuse or withdraw.
  • Non-personalised ads, fraud prevention and purchase checks: our legitimate interest in running and funding a free game, and providing the purchase you made.

6. Children

The game is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has provided information through the game, contact us and we will help remove it.

7. How long information is kept

Information on your device stays until you delete the game. Google and RevenueCat keep information according to their own privacy policies, linked above.

8. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct or delete personal information, to object to its use, and to withdraw consent. Because we don't hold any information that identifies you, most requests are best made directly to Google or RevenueCat, but you are welcome to contact us and we'll help. In the UK you can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new "last updated" date.
